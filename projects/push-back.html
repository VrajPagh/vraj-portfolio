01 · The Challenge

Designing for the game

Push Back demanded a robot that could collect blocks objects quickly, score them quickly, store several at once, and be able to descore opponent blocks. Our design therefore centered on speed adn constincey. My first impressiosn of the game is that it was heavy driver game therefore I would need a lot of practice de-scoring and scoring blocks.

Game overview

Turn field strategy into mechanical requirements

The game used red and blue scoring blocks and multiple field objectives. A successful robot needed to intake from the floor and loading areas, control objects inside the robot, and score into different goals without sacrificing mobility or reliability.

Fast acquisition Collect objects from varied approach angles.
High capacity Hold and index multiple objects.
Flexible scoring Reach more than one scoring objective.
Match durability Survive repeated impacts and long events.

02 · Engineering Process

Built through iteration

The final robot was not one design built once. Each prototype answered a specific engineering question, exposed weaknesses, and guided the next revision.

Step 01 · Drive prototype

Establishing a rigid foundation

The season began with a compact drivetrain used to validate packaging, wheel placement, chassis stiffness, and the space available for future scoring mechanisms.

Design focus: rigidity, low center of gravity, accessibility, and predictable handling.

Step 02 · First integrated prototype

Testing the full object path

The first complete prototype combined intake, vertical transport, storage, and scoring. It was intentionally experimental and helped identify transition points where objects could stall, shift, or jam.

Key lesson: the intake, conveyor, and storage system had to be designed as one continuous path.

Step 03 · CAD development

Resolving problems before fabrication

The competition design was modeled in Fusion 360 to verify mechanism geometry, structural mounting points, chain routing, component clearance, and overall packaging.

CAD advantage: interference and serviceability issues could be corrected before rebuilding physical assemblies.

03 · Mechanisms

One continuous scoring system

The robot used several linked subsystems. Each mechanism had to transfer the game object cleanly to the next stage while remaining compact enough for the complete robot.

Stage two conveyor

Controlled vertical indexing

Chain-mounted paddles maintained positive contact with each object and carried it vertically through the robot. Paddle placement was tuned to preserve spacing and reduce stalls.

Three-stage scoring

Moving objects from intake to release

Multiple driven stages created a continuous path from acquisition to final scoring, allowing the robot to process objects without requiring separate manual repositioning.

Pneumatic scorer

Fast, repeatable deployment

A pneumatic actuator provided rapid motion for the mid-goal scoring mechanism while keeping the system compact and mechanically simple.

Match loader scraper

Collecting from a fixed loading area

The scraper geometry was developed to guide objects away from the loader and into the intake path with minimal alignment from the driver.

04 · Mid Season

From prototype to proven competition robot

By mid-season, the early concepts had evolved into a reliable competition platform. The redesign focused on higher scoring throughput, better mechanism consistency, easier service access, and autonomous performance that could hold up under tournament pressure.

Competition validation

Mid-Season Robot and Awards

This version of the robot converted months of testing into measurable results. It earned tournament and robot-skills recognition while serving as the platform for our highest-scoring autonomous skills run.

Complete redesign

Competition-ready integration

The mid-season rebuild brought the intake, conveyor, storage path, scoring system, pneumatics, and drivetrain together into a more reliable package. The robot was designed around fast object flow, repeated match use, and rapid repairs between rounds.

Object transport

Three-stage conveyor

Multiple chain-driven stages maintained contact with game objects as they moved from intake to scoring. The system was refined to improve spacing, reduce jams, and allow rapid processing of several objects during a match.

Pneumatic mechanism

Fast mid-goal deployment

A pneumatic actuator powered the mid-goal mechanism, creating a fast and repeatable motion without adding another continuously driven motor. The linkage and polycarbonate guides were adjusted to improve alignment and release consistency.

Internal packaging

Serviceability and weight distribution

Batteries, motors, pneumatics, gearing, and wiring were packaged inside a compact frame while remaining accessible for maintenance. This layout supported consistent handling and allowed damaged or loose components to be reached quickly during tournament days.

07 · Reflection

What the season taught me

This project reinforced that competitive engineering is not about finding one perfect idea. It is about building quickly, testing honestly, identifying failure points, and improving the design until performance becomes repeatable. The final robot succeeded because every major subsystem was revised in response to real testing.
