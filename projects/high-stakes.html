01 · The Challenge

Designing for a changing match

High Stakes required a robot that could acquire game objects quickly, control them through the robot, score consistently, and remain durable through repeated matches. The design also needed to support autonomous routines and continued mechanism changes throughout the season.

Fast acquisitionCollect game objects without perfect alignment.
Controlled scoringKeep objects stable through every mechanism.
Reliable structureSurvive impacts and tournament use.
Continuous iterationAdapt the design as strategy changed.

02 · Development

Four builds, one evolving design

Each robot version tested a different combination of geometry, mechanism placement, object control, and structural support. The strongest decisions from each build were carried into the next version.

Prototype 01

Establishing the first layout

The first build combined the drivetrain and scoring concept into a testable robot, revealing packaging limits, weight-distribution issues, and weak object transitions.

Purpose: validate the overall architecture.

Prototype 02

Improving mechanism alignment

The second version adjusted mechanism positions and structure to create smoother object movement and a more stable frame.

Focus: better alignment with less complexity.

Prototype 03

Testing the full object path

This build tested the intake and scoring stages as one system. Repeated practice exposed issues that did not appear when mechanisms were tested separately.

Lesson: transitions matter as much as individual mechanisms.

Prototype 04

Competition refinement

The final prototype emphasized consistency, structural support, repair access, and repeatable performance under tournament conditions.

Priority: reliability over unnecessary complexity.

03 · Final Robot

The completed configuration

The final robot combined the strongest elements of the earlier prototypes into one competition-ready design shaped by testing, driver feedback, and reliability requirements.

Final Competition Robot

Designed through iteration

The completed design improved mechanism alignment, structural rigidity, object control, and service access while remaining compact enough for competition.

Intake

Refined to collect game objects from varied angles and reduce the precision required from the driver.

Scoring system

Developed as a connected path so every stage transferred objects cleanly into the next mechanism.

Competition structure

Reinforced while keeping motors, fasteners, and frequently serviced parts accessible.

05 · Reflection

What the project taught me

High Stakes showed me that strong engineering comes from testing ideas honestly and redesigning mechanisms that are not reliable enough.

The season strengthened my ability to compare tradeoffs, communicate design changes, work within tight packaging constraints, and prioritize consistency over unnecessary complexity. Every prototype became evidence for the next design rather than a finished product.
